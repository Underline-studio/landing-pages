# Anchor — Brand Voice Guide

## Positioning sentence
Anchor is an independent paid-scoping practice for utilities, IOUs, ISOs, and transmission owners implementing CAD/AI grid software. We turn an ambition into a vendor-ready statement of work in 30 to 60 days for a fixed $75,000.

## Voice in three words
**Operator. Specific. Quiet.**

- **Operator** — we have run the meetings the buyer is about to run. We don't narrate the industry to them.
- **Specific** — every claim points at a number, a standard, a project name, or a named artifact. No "leverage," no "synergies," no "transformation."
- **Quiet** — we are not louder than the buyer's problem. The voice carries the weight of someone who has shipped, not someone who is selling.

## Sentence shape
Short. Declarative. One clause carrying one fact. Periods do work that conjunctions can't.

When a sentence is long, it's because a number, a standard, or a named project earned the length.

## Do lexicon
- scoping memo, reference architecture, build/buy, statement of work, RFP-ready
- GRIP Round 2, CIP-013-3, FERC Order 1920, SPARK, EMS, ADMS, GIS, JTIQ
- ship, land, sign, deliver, decide
- engagement, deliverable, milestone, fixed fee
- "the second-best option and why it lost"
- "in language your steering committee will sign"

## Don't lexicon
- transform, leverage, synergy, holistic, robust, seamless, world-class, best-in-class, end-to-end (when used decoratively), unlock, empower, journey
- AI-powered, GenAI, next-gen, cutting-edge, revolutionary, game-changing
- "we partner with you" (we're hired by you)
- "trusted advisor" (earn it, don't claim it)
- emojis in client-facing copy
- exclamation points outside of subject lines that are clearly informal

## What Anchor says
- "$75,000 fixed. 30 to 60 days. One engagement at a time."
- "Half on signature, half on delivery."
- "We document the second-best option and why it lost."
- "References under NDA after the first call."
- "If we're not the right fit, you get three firms who might be, on one page."

## What Anchor doesn't say
- "Let's hop on a quick call to explore synergies." (replace: "30 minutes, no cost, either we fit or we don't.")
- "Our team of experts." (Anchor is one operator. Say so.)
- "We'll transform your grid planning." (replace: "We'll deliver a memo your steering committee can sign.")
- "Industry-leading methodology." (Don't claim category leadership without category proof.)
- Any reference to Bcal Energy, Amarnath Foundation, fuel cells, hospitals, or non-grid-software work. Strict brand isolation.

## What Anchor never claims
- That Anchor has shipped specific deliverables it hasn't.
- Logos of clients without written permission.
- Numbers (savings, time-to-decision, etc.) attributed to engagements that haven't closed.
- Affiliations with DOE, NERC, FERC, or any utility.

## Sample subject lines (cold)
- "Scoping the 389M before the SOW goes out"
- "Build/buy memo for Clean Path NY"
- "30-day scoping for the JTIQ Phase 2 portfolio"
- "$75K to land the CHARGE 2T scope"

## Tone calibration
If a sentence could come out of a Big-4 deck, rewrite it.
If a sentence could come out of a buyer's slack message to their boss, keep it.
