# Anchor — Pricing Rationale (Internal)

**Audience:** me, future advisors, future hires. Not for the client.
**Purpose:** keep me from caving on price in the first three calls and keep the next operator from re-pricing without re-deriving.

## The number
**$75,000 fixed. 30 to 60 days. One engagement at a time.**

Half on signature, half on delivery. Travel at cost.

---

## Why $75K, derived three ways

### 1. Bottoms-up: $/day rate at utility-consulting market clearing
- Senior strategy consultant at a Big-4 or boutique (Guidehouse, ICF, E3, Lux Research) bills out at **$2,400–$3,800/day** loaded.
- Anchor engagement is **30–45 working days** of effective senior time including discovery, drafting, vendor calls, and steering-committee adjustments.
- Floor: 30 days × $2,400 = **$72,000**. Ceiling: 45 days × $3,800 = **$171,000**.
- $75K sits one notch above the floor — defensible at the buyer's procurement desk against any benchmarking exercise, comfortably below the ceiling so the buyer never feels the boutique premium.

### 2. Comparable engagements (what the buyer's procurement team will Google)
| Firm | Comparable scoping engagement | Typical price |
|---|---|---|
| Guidehouse | Utility transmission planning advisory | $150K – $400K |
| E3 (Energy + Environmental Economics) | IRP / portfolio scoping | $200K – $600K |
| Lux Research | Tech build/buy memo | $80K – $180K |
| ICF | Grid modernization scoping | $150K – $300K |
| Quanta Technology | Engineering scoping (more technical) | $100K – $250K |
| Independent operator (us) | Anchor 30–60 day scoping | **$75K** |

Anchor is the **cheapest credible option** in the comparable set. That is intentional.

### 3. Buyer-frame: % of downstream procurement
- A grid-software implementation that justifies a paid scoping engagement is typically a **$3M–$15M** capex program.
- $75K is **0.5%–2.5%** of downstream spend. That is below the noise floor of a CFO's procurement review — it gets approved at director level, not committee level.
- This is the single most important framing: **$75K is a director-level signature, not a committee-level signature.** The price is set so the buyer can say yes without convening a sourcing event.

## What we leave on the table at $75K vs. $125K

| Lever | $75K | $125K |
|---|---|---|
| Director-level approval | Yes | Sometimes |
| Comparable to boutique floor | Floor | Mid-range |
| First-engagement objection volume | Low | Medium |
| Margin on a 35-day engagement | ~$1,800/day net | ~$3,200/day net |
| Time-to-close | 2–4 weeks typical | 4–10 weeks typical |
| Reference-build velocity | Fast (3–4 engagements/year) | Slower (2–3 engagements/year) |

**At $75K we trade ~$50K of per-engagement margin for roughly 2x the close rate and faster reference accumulation.** Year one this is the right trade. Year two, after three named references, repricing to $125K is the explicit move.

## What we leave on the table at $75K vs. $50K

- $50K reads as "a freelancer sent an invoice." Buyers in the GRIP-awardee tier discount cheap proposals — not because they want to overpay but because their procurement team is trained to. $75K is the lowest number that doesn't trigger that reflex.

---

## Anti-discount rules (read before any negotiation call)

1. **Never discount the price.** Discount the scope. If a buyer pushes back on $75K, remove the reference-architecture appendix or the vendor-shortlist call cycle, not the dollars.
2. **Never offer hourly.** Hourly invites scope creep and resets the buyer's mental model from "engagement" to "vendor." We are a fixed-fee practice.
3. **Never bundle.** Two engagements at a discount is two engagements at full price done back-to-back. The constraint is calendar, not cash.
4. **Never quote a range.** "$75K" is a price. "$60–90K" is an invitation to negotiate down.
5. **Travel is at cost, not bundled.** Buyers respect this; it signals operator-not-vendor.

## When to break the rules

- If a logo is unambiguously top-3 ICP (e.g., National Grid lead from the Power Up New England consortium) and they want a 30-day pilot at $50K with explicit reference rights, that is **a marketing spend, not a discount.** Take it once. Document why.
- Never break the rules twice in the same quarter.

## Repricing triggers (forward-looking)

| Trigger | New price |
|---|---|
| 3 named references with permission to use logo | $95K |
| 5 named references + one published case study | $125K |
| Waitlist >2 engagements | $150K + 50% deposit |
| Productized arm of practice raises seed | Anchor scoping becomes loss-leader for productized offering — re-evaluate model entirely |

---

## Bottom line

$75K is the price that gets a director to say yes in two weeks instead of getting a committee to say maybe in two months. Year-one volume + reference-building beats year-one margin. Re-derive when 3 references land.
